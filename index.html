Doctor Sawit AI

Multi-channel agronomy chatbot · PT. Digital Sawit Pro

role: AI & Data Analyst Intern period: Dec 2025 – Jul 2026 channels: Telegram · WhatsApp · Lark · Android
the problem

Farmers needed fast, trustworthy agronomy answers on the apps they already use — not a new one to learn. Every answer had to trace back to a real source instead of the model improvising, and the same logic had to run consistently across very different messaging platforms.

technical approach

Built as a retrieval-grounded system, not a prompt-and-hope chatbot: questions are matched against a structured agronomy knowledge base before the LLM drafts an answer. I structured that knowledge base by crop-health topic and designed the retrieval + conversation workflow in Dify, including when the bot answers directly vs. escalates to a human agronomist.

challenges & decisions

challenge

One chatbot logic, three messaging platforms with different formats and constraints.

decision

Kept the workflow channel-agnostic in Dify and containerized it with Docker, so adapters sit on top without duplicating logic.

challenge

A fluent-but-wrong answer is worse than no answer for real crop-health decisions.

decision

Grounded every response in the knowledge base instead of open-ended generation, and gated releases on faithfulness/recall scores.

Palm Tree Detection & Labeling — QGIS

Vision dataset validation for plantation monitoring · PT. Digital Sawit Pro

role: AI & Data Analyst Intern period: Dec 2025 – Jul 2026 tools: QGIS · YOLO detections
the problem

Raw YOLO detections on drone orthomosaic imagery weren't clean enough to use directly — false positives from ground texture and misses under canopy shadow needed manual review before the data could support tree-count and health analysis.

what I did

Loaded 5cm orthomosaic imagery and raw YOLO detections into QGIS alongside the plantation boundary layer, then manually validated each tree — correcting false positives, adding misses, and cross-checking coverage against the boundary to produce a clean, analysis-ready vector layer.

Lung Disease Detection with SHAP

Undergraduate thesis · UIN SUSKA Riau

role: Sole researcher period: 2025 data: 3,030 chest X-ray images
the problem

Deep-learning models for medical imaging often work as black boxes — accurate, but clinically risky without insight into why a prediction was made. The goal: a chest X-ray classifier accurate enough to be useful, with predictions a clinician could actually audit.

what I built

A VGG-16 model trained to classify chest X-rays into COVID-19, Normal, and Pneumonia, paired with SHAP to visualize which image regions drove each prediction — turning a black-box classifier into something a clinician could inspect.

  3. 03

    Applied SHAP to visualize which regions of each X-ray drove the model's prediction — inspectable, not just trusted.
